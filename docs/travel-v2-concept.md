# Travel v2: Concept

> Status: **concept for discussion, not a build plan.** Oct 1 2026.
> Built on the July whole-trip arc (`bdc4d167` and follow-ups). Its hard rules stay:
> - roleplay stops are performed line by line (`PerformedExchange`), never phrase tables;
> - never lighten a designed activity;
> - students contribute to Out & About, and vote on the destination;
> - Find Your Way's guide is always a student (the target is shown on the guide's phone only);
> - stages that must always work use deterministic content.

## What Travel is today

The sequence is:

**Boarding Call** (packing, climate-aware) → **Arrival** (immigration, performed) → **Getting There** (choose real transport, then the ticket desk, performed) → **Find Your Way** (directions on a real street map, micro) → **Hotel** (Conversation Rounds roleplay) → **Out & About** (Class Board per real attraction, then vote, then a travel moment) → **Local Table** (real menu, then ordering, performed) → **Trip Quiz** → **Trip Recap**.

**What's genuinely strong (keep):**
- Every city's real content: dishes, attractions, transport, images, coordinates, local customs, all 50 cities.
- Line-by-line performed dialogues with roles on real students; scripts tiered by level.
- Find Your Way's info-gap on a real map.
- The trip log feeding the recap and the journey.
- The repetition across cities as spaced practice.

## Honest problems

1. **Too long, and three stops feel the same.** It's 9 stages. Arrival, Getting There and Local Table all use the same performed-script engine, and Hotel is a fourth roleplay. By the third script, students are *reading* lines, not *living* a trip.
2. **Scripted all the way.** Every roleplay stays on the script. There's no moment where students handle the situation in their own words, which is the real skill a trip needs.
3. **No "you" in the trip.** Every student is a generic "traveller". Choices (transport, dish) are free and costless, so there's little to talk about ("which one?" with no reason to prefer one).
4. **No listening.** A real trip is full of listening: announcements, a waiter's question, directions. Travel has none.
5. **No before/after.** The recap retells the trip, but nothing shows what the class can now *do*.
6. **It doesn't fly from the Live Room.** Travel needs its city trip pack, so it only launches from the World Flight map, not from the room's destination.

## The idea: your trip, with a budget and a problem

**Each student travels as someone.** At boarding, every phone gets a **Traveller Card**:
- **who you are**: e.g. *a student backpacker*, *a family with a toddler*, *a food blogger*, *a business traveller*;
- **a budget** in the local currency;
- **one thing you want** from this city ("eat something you've never tried", "see the view from up high").

The card carries through the whole trip:
- **Choices now cost something.** A taxi or the metro? The tasting menu or street food? Students argue from *their* card ("I can't afford the taxi, I'm a backpacker"). That makes real talk out of the existing real data.
- **The trip log becomes personal.** "Mia (food blogger) took the metro, ate *takoyaki*, and saw Senso-ji."

## The flight: fewer stops, each one deeper

| Phase | Stage | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Boarding** | **Traveller Cards + Can-do check** | Phones get their card. Then a quick **"Could you do this in Tokyo?"** self-check (buy a ticket, order food, ask the way, check in). This is the **before**. |
| **Arrival** | **Immigration** (kept, performed) | As now, but the officer's questions use each traveller's card ("Purpose of your visit?" → their reason). |
| *Break* | **Announcement** (new, listening) | A real-sounding station/airport announcement for the city (Radio Check style, synthetic voice): "The train to Shinjuku leaves from platform 4…". Phones catch the platform, time and destination. |
| **3 chosen stops** | Teacher (or a class vote) picks **3 of 5**: Getting There · Find Your Way · Hotel · Out & About · Local Table | Each stop is **Take 1, scripted** (performed exchange, as now) **→ Take 2, a problem card**, played in your own words with a phrase cheat on the phone. For example: *the metro is closed*, *they've lost your booking*, *the dish has peanuts and you're allergic*. Take 2 is where the trip happens. |
| **Landing** | **Postcard Home + Can-do stamps** | Each student says a spoken **postcard home** (past tense, from their card: "Dear Mum, today in Tokyo I…"). Then the same can-do check: **stamps** for what the class can now do (the **after**), the trip log card, and the arrival scene. |

That's 5–6 stages instead of 9: more depth, less reading, and a real arc from scripted to unscripted.

## What carries through
- **The Traveller Card:** choices, problems, the postcard and the trip log.
- **Key phrases per stop:** they go into the phrasebook, the Phrase Hunt runs during the stops, and the postcard reuses them.
- **The can-do check:** boarding → landing stamps → the logbook.
- **The city:** the room's destination (Live Room) or the map's city (World Flight). It's the same trip pack either way.

## Travel in the Live Room
The room already knows the destination, so Travel is offered in the Flight plan panel and builds its trip pack for **that** city (the same data the map launch uses). Flying to Tokyo and choosing Travel means the trip is in Tokyo.

## New pieces (none decided)
1. **Traveller Cards:** personas × budgets × wants; per-student phone card; choices show cost vs budget.
2. **Problem cards (Take 2)** for each stop, plus the phone phrase cheat.
3. **The Announcement** listening break: city-specific, built from transport data, synthetic voice.
4. **Choose 3 of 5 stops** (teacher pick or class vote).
5. **Postcard Home** (spoken) + **can-do check/stamps** (before/after).
6. **Travel in the room's Flight plan panel** (trip pack built for the room's destination).

## Questions for you
1. **Traveller Cards with a budget:** fun and useful for your classes, or one thing too many?
2. **3 of 5 stops:** teacher picks, class votes, or both (teacher picks the shortlist)?
3. **Take 2 problem cards:** right level of challenge? (A fallback could be a gentler "change one detail" Take 2.)
4. **Hotel:** keep it as Conversation Rounds, or bring it onto the same performed-script engine as the others?
5. **The postcard:** spoken only, or spoken plus a one-line written version for the logbook?
