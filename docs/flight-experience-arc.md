# The flight as one experience (planning, 29 Oct 2026)

Claude's draft for discussion with Matt. Nothing here is built yet unless it says "exists". The aim: a lesson feels like
one journey with a story arc, like a good game level: anticipation, departure, a calm middle with surprises, a climax,
and a reward that makes the class want the next flight.

## The arc in six acts

| Act | Feeling | Lesson time | What exists | What's missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Gate | Anticipation, "we're going somewhere" | Arrival, check-in chat | Outside camera at the runway, boarding QR, boarding passes on phones, city vote | A boarding *moment*: names appearing as each student boards (walk-up / seat filling), "doors closing" when the teacher is ready |
| 2. Departure | Lift-off thrill | First stage begins | Departure cinematic, climb, departure landmark after the climb | A countdown or "cleared for take-off" beat that the class shares; the view breaking through the cloud tops into sun (the first "wow") |
| 3. Cruise | Calm, ambient, the world turning below | Most activities | Cockpit cruise, scenery, landmarks on route, turbulence beat, ISS, natural events, weather | Pacing: the cruise is one long stretch. Needs *waypoints*: small arrival-style title cards as the plane crosses a border, sea or big city ("Now over the Persian Gulf"), and a gentle return to the view between activities |
| 4. Landmark moments | Wonder, "look!" | Between activities | Landmarks drawn in the scenery, photo mode (photo chance → take photo) | The special treatment: plane slows, gentle bank toward it, light warms, title card, photo chance; then it slides behind and the flight resumes. One per flight is the hero, others are smaller |
| 5. Descent and landing | The climax | Last stage → wrap-up | Descent announcement, cloud dive, reveal, arrival touchdown, branch slide-in, Welcome card (delayed, spring) | **The landing turn** (bank over the city so the class sees it whole), the city revealed through the clouds as a beat, wheels-down sound/feel, taxi to the gate. Then a clear "we have arrived" pause before any UI |
| 6. Arrival and reward | Pride, collecting | Wrap-up | Welcome card, souvenirs, postcards, stamps, logbook, next-city vote | A reward *sequence* instead of separate pieces: stamp lands in the passport → souvenir revealed → postcard written → "where next?" vote as the cliffhanger. Each beat a short animation, teacher taps to advance |

## Transitions that need work (beyond landing turn and landmarks)

1. **Activity in / activity out.** Today an activity covers the view and then disappears. It should feel like the cabin:
   the view dims and blurs behind a card that slides up like a tray table; on close, the card slides away and the view
   comes back into focus with a small beat ("Back to the window"). This happens many times per lesson, so it matters most.
2. **Gate → takeoff.** The click from the outside camera to the cockpit should be one continuous move (camera pulls into
   the cockpit as the plane rolls), not a cut.
3. **Climb → cruise.** The cloud break-through: grey, then bright, then the sea of clouds and sun. One clear wow early.
4. **Time of day.** When the flight crosses sunset or sunrise, mark it softly (a gentle colour shift plus a small caption,
   "Sunset over Iran"). It already happens in the sky; it isn't named.
5. **Waypoint cards.** Poster-style title cards for borders, seas, deserts and mountain ranges, fading in and out over a
   few seconds, in the poster type of the art guide. Good for teaching (geography in passing) and pacing.
6. **Turbulence.** Exists; add the "seatbelt sign" chime and icon as the start and end marker.
7. **Descent start.** "We're beginning our descent" as a cabin moment: the light changes, the seatbelt sign comes on, a
   short pause, the clouds come up. The class should feel the shift from activity to arrival.
8. **Arrival → next city.** The next-city vote is the cliffhanger. Present it like a departures board flipping, ending on
   the vote, so the class leaves wanting the next flight.
9. **Ending without landing.** Needs a gentle version too ("We'll continue next time"), with the plane holding over the
   clouds, so a short class still ends on a nice picture.

## Sound (light touch, mutable)

A small set of cues tie the arc together: boarding chime, engines spooling, the seatbelt chime, a soft wind bed in
cruise, a landmark sting, touchdown, and the stamp thud. Volume low; one switch; off by default when testing.

## Motion rules (from the art guide)

Slow and smooth; no pops. Cards slide and spring gently; title cards fade. The teacher screen is projected, so motion
must read on Zoom: no fast flicker, nothing that shimmers at low frame rates.

- Atmospheric moments: lean in. Rainbows when conditions are real (sun low behind the plane, rain opposite: needs the weather system), also glory, sun rays, dusk city glow.
