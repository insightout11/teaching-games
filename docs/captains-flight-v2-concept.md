# Captain's Flight v2: Concept

> Status: **concept for discussion, not a build plan.** Oct 1 2026.
> Designed fresh: nothing is kept just because it exists. Earlier review: `docs/captains-flight-improvements.md` (Jul 2026).

## What it has to be

The **signature lesson**: the one teachers try first and come back to, and the one that shows what LessonCaptain is. "A bit of everything": a real source, new language, real talk, a game, and a moment of reflection. Every skill appears once, at its best moment, and the whole flight feels like **one journey**, not eight activities.

## What's wrong with today's version (honest)

- **Too many beats, none of them the star.**
  - The sequence is a warm-up, the briefing, an opinion micro, the Toolkit, an accuracy micro, the discussion, a navigation micro, the review game and a wrap-up.
  - That's 9 beats, each a different mechanic. A teacher remembers none of them as *the* moment.
- **No through-line.** The prediction at takeoff, the opinion pulse, the discussion and the Final Word are four separate questions about the topic. Nothing gets answered by the end.
- **The briefing is passive.** Students watch or read, *then* take a quiz. Attention drops during the source, which is the most important input of the lesson.
- **The accuracy micro and navigation micro** are filler beats picked from pools, unrelated to the source. The navigation micro is still Radar Fix, which is retired.
- **The landing is thin.** The Final Word is one prompt. The lesson's last impression is its weakest beat.

## The idea: one Flight Question

**Every Captain's Flight is built around one big question that the source helps answer.**
> *"Should cities ban cars?" · "Why do we dream?" · "Is it ever OK to lie?" · "Could humans live on Mars?"*

- **Takeoff:** the question is asked, and everyone commits to a first answer.
- **The middle:** each stage hands the class something for the question: evidence from the source, the words to argue with, a chance to argue it.
- **Landing:** the same question again, and the class sees how its thinking changed.

This is what makes it *the* signature lesson: **a question, investigated**. It's also the cleanest before/after there is: "We started at 40% yes. We landed at 70% yes, and here's why."

The question is generated from the source (or the topic). The teacher sees it at setup and can rewrite it in one line.

## The flight: 6 beats + 2 short breaks

| Phase | Beat | What happens | What it hands on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Boarding** (while students join) | **The Flight Question** | The question fills the screen. Phones: **What do you think?** (agree ↔ disagree) and **What will the source say?** (pick one of 3 predictions). Two taps; no reveal yet. | The "before": stances + predictions |
| **Climb** | **The Briefing, with a mission** | Watch or read the source. Phones show **3 things to catch** while it plays (a fact, a number, a reason), tapped as they hear them. No quiz afterwards: the mission *is* the comprehension. Then the **prediction reveal**: "Most of you predicted X. The source said Y." | Evidence the class caught; what surprised them |
| **Climb** | **Words for the Question** | 5–6 key phrases from the source (Language Toolkit, spoken), framed as *the words you'll need to argue it*. Each phrase gets a quick "say it" round. | The phrases → into everyone's phrasebook |
| *Turbulence* | *A 2-minute break* | Something fast and silly, connected to the source (Static, Would You Rather, a two-question quiz). The flight hits turbulence; seatbelts on. | Energy |
| **Cruise** | **The Big Discussion** (the main event) | The class argues the Flight Question. The **format fits the question** (below). Phones show the phrases, plus a **Phrase Hunt**: "use 2 of today's phrases" with stamps (Grammar Hunt, for phrases). | Arguments, who changed their mind, phrase stamps |
| *Turbulence* | *A 2-minute break* | Optional; the teacher's call. | |
| **Descent** | **The Review Game** | A team game built from the source and the class's struggles. The class **votes the route** from 3 games. | Fun; the misses come back |
| **Landing** | **The Verdict** | The Flight Question again. Reveal: **the Shift** (start vs now) + **prediction vs reality** + **one spoken Final Word each** ("I used to think…, now I think…"). Lands in the city (World Flight). | The logbook entry |

The result is 6 real beats, each with a clear job, plus breaks that feel like part of the flight.

### The Big Discussion: the format fits the question

| The Flight Question is… | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A **yes/no or "should…?"** question | **Hot Take Arena** (upgraded): stances, hear both sides, the verdict vote | A natural fit for taking sides |
| A **"how could we…?" problem** | **Decision Council**: proposals, challenge, vote | Builds solutions together |
| A **personal / "what would you…?"** question | **Conversation Rounds**: partners, roles, goals | Talk, not debate |
| A **big class with time to spare** | **Team Debate** | The full structured debate |

The generator classifies the question (we already classify sources by genre). The teacher can switch formats in one tap.

### The Verdict (landing)
1. The question returns. Phones re-answer it (one tap).
2. **The Shift:** start-of-lesson vs now, "6 of 14 changed their mind".
3. **Prediction vs reality:** what the class expected the source to say, and what it actually said.
4. **Final Word:** each student says one sentence aloud, with a starter on the phone: *"I used to think…, but now…"* / *"The most surprising thing was…"*. Turns go fewest-first, using the speaking engine.
5. **The logbook card:** "Flight 12 · Should cities ban cars? · 40% → 70% yes · Top phrase: 'traffic jam' · Lisbon".

## What carries through (the thread)

- **The Flight Question** carries the whole flight.
- **Key phrases** go from the Briefing, to the Words, to the Discussion (Phrase Hunt), to the review game, to the logbook.
- **Predictions** go from Boarding to the Briefing reveal to the Verdict.
- **Stances** go from Boarding to the Verdict (the Shift).
- **Struggles** (missed catches, wrong answers) go to the review game.
- **Course carry-over:** the next lesson's warm-up recalls last flight's question and phrases.

| Today | v2 |

| Briefing + a quiz after | **Briefing with a mission** (catch it while it plays) |
| Opinion pulse micro | **Folded into Boarding** (the stance tap) |

## New pieces to build (none of it is decided)

1. The Flight Question: generated with the lesson, editable at setup, classified by type.
2. Boarding taps: stance + prediction (reusing Quick Pulse/Prediction mechanics, on the lobby screen).
3. Briefing missions: "catch it" phone targets during the video or reading (Radio Check's question style, taps instead of a quiz).
4. Phrase Hunt: Grammar Hunt generalised to the lesson's phrases when there's no grammar target.
5. Discussion format routing by question type.
6. The Verdict landing.
7. Turbulence breaks: the turbulence beat already exists at /dev/turbulence, waiting for your approval of how it feels.

## Questions for you

1. **The Flight Question as the identity:** does "a question, investigated" feel like *your* signature lesson?
2. **The briefing with a mission instead of a quiz after:** agree?
3. **Discussion format:** should it be auto-picked by question type (teacher can switch), or should the teacher always choose?
4. **Turbulence breaks:** one fixed break, or a teacher-placed "turbulence" button they can hit any time?
5. **Anything from today's version you'd fight to keep?** (For example, Decision Council always, or the accuracy micro.)

## Decisions (Oct 1 2026)
All five questions answered yes; turbulence = both (one default break + a button any time); nothing from v1 kept for its own sake.

## Built (Oct 1 2026)

Captain's Flight now: Flight Question → Briefing (mission) → Language Toolkit → Turbulence → the Big Discussion → review game (class vote) → the Verdict.
