# Content sweep and stand-in audit, 12 Oct 2026

Two checks, run to find content bugs before a class does. Nothing was changed as a result: this is a report to
choose from.

## What was run
- **The sweep.** 44 activities, each generated by the real AI for three topics at three levels: "Rainbows and
  Unicorns" (Easy), "Food chains" (Intermediate), "The burial practices of Trunyan in Kintamani, Bali" (Advanced).
  132 runs, about ten minutes. No material (video or text) was attached, so this tests lessons built from a topic
  alone. An automatic checker flagged suspicious output, and I read the output behind every flag.
- **The stand-in audit.** A search of the code for content that is swapped in when the AI fails, to find stand-ins
  that ignore the lesson's topic.

**Result:** 85 of 132 runs were clean. 47 were flagged; after reading them, 5 real problems and a set of false
alarms. Not covered: lessons built from a video or text, the games that generate inside themselves (Flash Quiz,
Grid Rush, Connections and others), Junior mode, and anything about how a screen looks or plays.

---

## Real problems, most serious first

### 1. The ready-made banks match the wrong topic (Speak and Debate lessons)
A lesson on the Trunyan burial practices got:
- **Situation Check:** "In a future-city design contest, your team invents a smart bus shelter…"
- **Motion Pulse, Evidence Cards, Tag-Team Debate:** "Schools should practice peer decisions through team projects"

No AI was involved: these came from the hand-checked banks, picked by a word match. The match counts small common
words. The topic starts with "The", and the bus-shelter situation is tagged "cities of **the** future", so it
matched. Any topic containing "the" (or another filler word found in a tag) can pull in an unrelated situation or
motion, and because the bank wins over the AI, the whole Speak or Debate lesson is then built on the wrong subject.

"Food chains" also got a compost motion ("Every school should compost food scraps") on the strength of the word
"food": related, but not the lesson's topic.

**Fix:** ignore filler words when matching, and require a match on the topic's distinctive words, otherwise let
the AI write it. Small change, high value. This is the same family as the off-topic bugs you hit this week.

### 2. Cargo Hold jokes about a real community's funeral rites
For the Trunyan topic the new, funnier card writing produced "a ritual waffle drenched in maple syrup", "the sacred
burrito floating above the altar", "the decomposing banana hidden under the sofa". The humour rules ban a list of
things (violence, bodily humour and so on) but say nothing about religion, death, or a real people's customs.

**Fix:** add a rule: when the topic is a real culture, religion, death, illness or tragedy, the jokes come from
everyday school and travel life and never from the beliefs, rites or people themselves. Consider the same rule for
every activity that is told to be funny. For some topics the right answer may be to not offer Cargo Hold at all.

### 3. Cargo Hold on a narrow topic uses the same two words on every card
"Rainbows and Unicorns": 17 cards built on "rainbow" and 15 on "unicorn", so every card's meaning is one of two
sentences. The cards themselves were good ("a unicorn on skates", "seven rainbow cupcakes") and the six sentence
frames all worked. But a vocabulary game that teaches two words is thin.

**Fix:** require at least 12 different lesson words per deck, drawing on the topic's wider world (colours, weather,
myths, horses) when the topic itself is two words.

### 4. Say It Again drifts off the topic
"Food chains" produced "What is your favourite meal, and how does your family usually prepare it together?" The
activity asks for one personal question and the AI wandered from food chains to food. One question is the whole
activity, so a miss here is a miss for the stage.

**Fix:** tell it the question must be answerable only by someone who has done this lesson, and name the topic's
key idea in it.

### 5. Advanced-level wording is beyond most teenage learners
Hot Seat meanings such as "a colossal, sacred tree whose expansive canopy conceals the deceased from putrefaction",
and Quick-fire questions such as "How does the concept of eternal rest shape your cultural perspective?" These obey
the "Advanced" level as written. Whether that is what you want from a spoken, fast activity is your call.

**Fix (if you agree):** cap spoken, timed activities one level below the class level, since students must answer
aloud in seconds.

## Smaller things
- **Cargo Hold sentence frames have no full stop.** The AI left the ending blank, so sentences read aloud fine but
  show without a final full stop. One-line fix.
- **Decision Council sends an empty "details from the material" list** when there is no material. Worth checking
  the screen does not show an empty box.
- **Scene Igniter is the slowest activity** (about 4 to 5 seconds, four scenes at once) after Cargo Hold (6 to 8
  seconds). Both are within reason.

## False alarms (checked, nothing wrong)
- **"May be off-topic"** on Imposter, Quick-fire, Compare It, Fix the Captain, Scenario Simulator, Flight Verdict
  and others: the checker looks for the topic's own words, and good content often uses related words instead
  (predator, hawk, meadow for "Food chains"). Read and fine.
- **Two Truths and a Lie, Opinion Shift, Wonder Board, Final Word:** flagged for "very little content". By design:
  students supply the content.
- **Story Sprint:** flagged as failed. It is a game that generates inside itself, so it was the wrong thing to ask
  this generator for.
- **Speak, Debate "no AI call"** for Rainbows and Food chains: the bank was used, and for those two it was on or
  near the topic (see problem 1 for when it is not).

## What looked good
Fact Detective, Language Toolkit, Conversation Rounds, Decision Council, Fix the Captain, Pass the Line, Scene
Igniter, Imposter and Scenario Simulator all produced on-topic, level-appropriate content for all three topics.
Scene Igniter returned real scripts every time (the fix from earlier today held across 12 scenes).

## Stand-in audit: what a class sees when the AI fails
59 stand-ins found; 35 use the lesson's topic. These do not, and would show unrelated content:

| Where | What the class would get |
| --- | --- |
| **Imposter** | Classroom words ("a room in a school where students gather to learn") whatever the topic |
| **Taboo Sprint / Hot Seat** | Generic words ("to share information or ideas with others") |
| **Bluff Definition** | Three fixed words: lexicon, syntax, nuance |
| **Flash Quiz** | A fixed grammar quiz ("Which sentence uses the present perfect correctly?") |
| **Sector Strike** | A question "Which of the following is true about <topic>?" with four fixed nonsense answers ("It was invented last year", "It only exists in one country"). The "correct" one is "It is commonly studied and discussed" |
| **Design mission** | A generic round ("Make it easier to use / more adaptable / fairer"). Usable, but the same every time |
| **Class talk follow-up** | "What else can you tell us about your opinion?" |

Password and Interview Lab also have generic stand-ins, but both are retired or hidden.

Taboo Sprint, Hot Seat and Twenty Questions now have real decks for 150 topics, so their stand-ins could use those
first. Sector Strike's is the worst: it presents a made-up "fact" as the right answer.

**None of these tell anyone they happened.** A teacher cannot tell stand-in content from a bad AI answer, and
neither can I afterwards. That is what item 3 of the plan (recording every stand-in and error in a live lesson)
would fix.

---

## Suggested order
1. Fix the bank matching (problem 1). Small, and it removes a whole class of off-topic lessons.
2. Add the sensitivity rule for humour (problem 2).
3. Replace the Sector Strike and Flash Quiz stand-ins, and point Taboo, Hot Seat and Imposter at the decks.
4. Record stand-ins and errors from live lessons.
5. Cargo Hold word variety, Say It Again, the level cap for spoken activities (your call on the last).
6. Extend the sweep to lessons with a video or text, and to the self-generating games.

## Running it again
```bash
set -a && . /path/to/.env.local && set +a && CONTENT_SWEEP=1 pnpm vitest run src/__tests__/sweep
```
It is skipped in the normal test run. `SWEEP_ONLY=cargo-hold,imposter` limits it to some activities, and
`SWEEP_OUT=file.json` chooses where the full output goes. It makes real AI calls, so it costs a little each run.
